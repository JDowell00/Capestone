## 🚨 Phishing Email Red Flags

Use this quick guide to identify potential phishing emails. This detector is not perfect it will make mistakes and inaccurate guesses. It is still a good idea to be aware and knowledgeable of the different types of methods attackers may use. 

### ⚠️ Urgency or Threats
Messages that pressure you to act immediately (e.g., “Your account will be locked in 24 hours”) are a common tactic.

### 📧 Suspicious Sender Address
Check the full email address, not just the display name. Look for misspellings or unusual domains.

### 👤 Generic Greetings
Phrases like “Dear Customer” instead of your actual name can indicate a mass phishing attempt.

### 🔗 Mismatched or Suspicious Links
Hover over links before clicking. If the URL looks different from the official site, do not click it.

### 🔒 Requests for Sensitive Information
Legitimate companies will NOT ask for passwords, SSNs, or banking details via email.

### 📎 Unexpected Attachments
Do not open attachments you weren’t expecting—especially `.zip`, `.exe`, or unknown files.

### ✍️ Poor Grammar and Formatting
Spelling errors or awkward phring can be a red flag.

### 💰 Too Good to Be True
Unexpected prizes, refunds, or money offers are often scams.

### 🎨 Inconsistent Branding
Logos, colors, or formatting that look slightly off from official emails.

### 🧾 Unusual Requests
Requests for gift cards, wire transfers, or bypassing normal procedures are major warning signs.

---

## ✅ Quick Safety Checklist

- ❌ Don’t click suspicious links  
- ❌ Don’t download unknown attachments  
- ✅ Verify through official websites  
- ✅ Report suspicious emails  
- ✅ Delete when unsure  

---

## 🔍 Pro Tip
If something feels off, trust your instincts. It’s better to double-check than fall for a phishing attack.
